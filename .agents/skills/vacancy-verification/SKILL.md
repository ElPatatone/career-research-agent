# Vacancy verification

Input is a candidate identity with possible routes, previous evidence and any unresolved question. Check the role identity before assigning evidence. Prefer the employer careers page and the ATS, form, mailbox or job-board application it authorises. Check the role-specific destination for evidence of current external intake. A generic careers address or an old job-board snippet alone is insufficient.

Record the inspected URL, check date, observed text or concise observation, source authority and limits of inspection. For each material term capture the source or mark it unknown. Look for location, working arrangement, workload, travel, essential experience, eligibility, paid status, full-time status, contract duration, salary and original posting date. Separate third-party claims and inferences from employer evidence. A search indexing date is distinct from a posting date.

| Intake | Evidence and downstream handling |
|---|---|
| open | Current authorised route explicitly accepts external applicants for this role. Capture a usable application link or employer-authorised email route. Flag any remaining candidate decision separately. |
| closed | Employer or authorised destination confirms that intake ended for this vacancy. Preserve the dated observation. |
| internal-only | Advert explicitly limits intake to internal candidates. Hold pending evidence of external intake. |
| inconclusive | Evidence is absent, inaccessible, conflicting or too weak to decide. Describe the gap. |

For a broken page, HTTP error, missing result or login wall, check other authorised routes such as the employer board and its linked ATS. An account requirement can coexist with clear evidence of open external intake. If intake remains uncertain, retain a promising role for follow-up. Avoid submitting forms, contacting employers or creating accounts to test intake.

Return the outcome, evidence, material changes since the prior check and outstanding questions. Distinguish an open route awaiting a user decision from unresolved intake, even when both lead to follow-up.
